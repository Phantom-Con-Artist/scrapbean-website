What is ScrapBean?

A scrapbook that lives on your computer or your phone. Think of a shoebox of photos, but with better manners. You put things in, it lays them out with tape, sticky notes and a slightly wonky tilt, and you get a page worth keeping.

No boxes to draw, no grids to line up. Add a little, shuffle a little, and see where it all lands.

  1. Dump it.

    Paste, drop or type. Photos, GIFs, videos, voice notes, links, half a thought. Don't be neat about it.

  2. Shuffle it.

    See where it lands this time. Press the dice and everything goes up in the air, to the sound of a deck of cards, and settles somewhere new. Press it again. Nobody is counting.

  3. Done.

    Read it like a book. Keep it for yourself, or send a copy to someone who was there. Your mum, probably.

Making a page

Make a page with your thumb

Write a note in the row at the bottom, or press + to take a photo, record a video or pick something already on your phone. The page lays itself out as things arrive.

Shuffle, one tap at a time

With nothing typed, the button beside the box shuffles. Every tap deals the whole page again, sizes, tilts and tape included.

Hold a scrap to change it

Hold anything on the page and a sheet comes up: shuffle its look, make it bigger or smaller, change the words or the caption, or take it off the page. Undo is there if you change your mind.

Paper and handwriting

The palette button opens the page look: the same backgrounds and handwriting as the computer, in two strips you scroll with a thumb. Or press Surprise me.

Looking back

Oh, that day. Scrapbooks you haven't opened for a while come back as Highlights; tap one and have another look.

Read it like a book

A scrapbook opens for reading. Swipe sideways to turn the page. Each page is dealt to fit the screen, with room below to scroll if there's more.

Highlights: the ones you forgot

Scrapbooks you have not opened for a few days come back along the top of the library, like stories. Tap one and its pages play by themselves, with its song if it has one. Tap to skip, hold to pause, pull down to close. Switch it off in Settings if you would rather not.

Find it by typing

The magnifying glass opens a search box. A name, a place, or any word written on a page, and the shelf narrows to the scrapbook you meant.

What stays on the computer

The phone app is the lighter of the two, on purpose. These are on the computer only, at least for now:

  • Plugins
  • Saving a copy as a web page, a PDF or a picture (the phone sends the scrapbook itself instead)
  • Importing your own fonts and stickers

Pages made on the computer look the same on the phone, threads, calendars and all. The two read and write the same kind of library.

Quick if you're busy. Deep if you're fussy.

Read it like a book. Add things when you have a moment: Edit makes a page in the time it takes the kettle to boil. Customize is there when you feel like fiddling, and for people with opinions about tape. Same scrapbook; switch whenever you like.

Reading, for everyone

Click a scrapbook and it opens like a book. Turn pages with the arrows. Nothing can be nudged, deleted or ruined, so it is safe to hand over to a relative.

Edit, for the casual

One row at the bottom and nothing to learn. Type a note, paste a photo, press the dice. ScrapBean picks the paper, the tape and the layout. A decent page takes about ten seconds.

Customize, for the particular

A notepad of options slides in beside the page. Place every scrap by hand, set a theme for the whole book, or click one thing and change only that. Nothing here is required.

How deep does Customize go? As deep as you do.

Keep the bits that belong together

The photo, the story behind it, and nine seconds of purring. You can stop at add and shuffle, but there is a whole drawer of extras for when you feel like fiddling.

Threads

Tie a thread from any scrap to any other, pin to pin: a note to a photo, a photo to a video. A few of those and your notes turn into a map of what belongs with what. Shuffle the page and tied scraps land side by side: different places, still together.

Calendar stickers

Stick a month on the page and circle the days that matter. Birthdays, flights, the day the plumber swore he'd come.

GIFs and videos that keep moving

Drop in a GIF and it loops on the page like a tiny television. Videos play when you read the scrapbook. Shuffle as much as you like; they carry on, and their threads hold.

Sound you can press

A voice note or a song becomes its own scrap: a cassette, a wave or a row of bars with a play button. Grandad's voicemail, rain on the shed roof, the baby's first opinion. Nothing plays until you press it.

A shelf that finds things

Every scrapbook stands on a shelf. Type a few letters and the shelf narrows to the one you meant. It reads what's written on the pages too, so "that note about the boiler" is findable. Tag a scrapbook "Vacation" and it gets a shelf of its own: type vacation and that shelf opens.

Five desks, day and night

Craft desk, Garden shed, Harbour, Keepsake tin and Workshop. Each one has a light side for the morning and a dark side for when you should be asleep.

Send someone a little book

Keep it for yourself, or send a copy to someone who was there. From your computer, save a copy they can open with nothing installed:

  • A web page

    The whole scrapbook in one zip. Unzip it, open index.html, and it opens in any browser. Videos play, sound plays, GIFs loop, threads stay tied.

  • A PDF

    One sheet per page, the same shape as on your screen. Good for printing, emailing to a teacher, or keeping somewhere safe.

  • A picture

    One page as a PNG. The quickest way into a group chat. Anything that moves is caught as a still, and you choose the moment.

There is no ScrapBean cloud and no share link. You send the file however you already send files, and it stays yours.

From your phone, the scrapbook itself

Hold it on the shelf and press Send to someone. It goes as one file, photos, videos, sounds and all, by WhatsApp, Bluetooth, email or however you like. Whoever gets it opens the file with ScrapBean and it lands on their shelf.

ScrapBean will cost nothing to download and nothing to keep using. There is no paid tier, no sync plan and no upgrade button waiting for you on page three.

Your scrapbooks are files on your own computer or phone. They stay there unless you send a copy, and they still open with the internet off.

The honest small print: ScrapBean is free, but it is not open source. The app's code stays in our drawer. Plugins are how you bolt on your own bits: anyone can write one, and they are plain JavaScript.

Plugins, kept in a box

A plugin is a small extra somebody else wrote. It adds a command to the menu: count the words on a page, stamp today's date, make a black-and-white copy of your photos. You add one from a folder, and take it out just as easily.

Other people's code on your computer deserves a little suspicion. So ScrapBean supplies it for you.

It does one thing, when you ask

A plugin sleeps until you press its command, gets fifteen seconds, and is switched off again the moment it finishes. Whatever it changed comes with one Undo.

It asks before it looks

The first time a plugin wants to read a page, see a picture or add something, you get a plain question that says exactly what it would see. "Not now" is a perfectly good answer.

You can change your mind

One page lists every plugin, what you have let it do, and a button to take each thing back. Switch it off, or remove it and everything it kept goes with it.

What a plugin cannot do, however politely it asks

  • Open or save files on your computer
  • Use the internet, at all
  • See pages you don't have open, unless you allow it
  • Delete anything
  • Run when you haven't pressed it
  • See what another plugin is doing

We didn't take this on trust. ScrapBean is tested against plugins written to break out, flood it, hang it and lie to it. How plugins work, and how to write one.

Questions people ask

Short answers. The long ones are in the documentation.

Is ScrapBean really free?

Yes. It will be free to download and free to use, and there is nothing to buy inside it. No trial, no paid tier, no subscription.

When can I get it, and for which computers?

Soon. ScrapBean is coming to Windows, macOS and Linux, and to Android phones on the same day. The downloads will appear on this page when they are ready. There is no iPhone or iPad version yet.

Is there a phone app?

Yes, for Android, arriving the same day as the computer app. It has the same pages, papers, stickers and handwriting, made for a thumb: hold a scrap to change it or drag it somewhere else, tie threads, sort scrapbooks onto shelves, swipe to turn the page, and take a photo straight onto the page. Plugins, your own fonts and stickers, and saving copies stay on the computer.

Do I need an account?

No. There is no sign-up, no email to confirm and no password to forget. Open the app and start pasting.

What can I put on a page?

Pictures, animated GIFs, videos (MP4, WebM, MOV), voice notes and songs, links, and notes you type. Then stickers, tape, pins, calendars and threads on top.

Do I have to arrange everything myself?

No. By default a page arranges itself to fit the screen, and Shuffle deals a new arrangement whenever you like, with the sound of a deck of cards. You can also make an endless page that runs on. If you want control, move scraps by hand: Customize on the computer, or hold and drag on the phone.

Where are my scrapbooks kept?

On your own computer or phone, in ScrapBean's own folder. ScrapBean keeps your library on your device, and nothing leaves it unless you choose to send a copy. On the computer, the app only goes online to fetch a picture you drag in from a web page.

Can I send a scrapbook from my phone?

Yes. Hold the scrapbook on the shelf and press Send to someone. It goes as one file with its photos, videos and sounds, by WhatsApp, Bluetooth, email, or any app you send files with. Whoever gets it opens the file with ScrapBean, and the scrapbook goes on their shelf as a copy of their own.

Can I send a scrapbook to someone who doesn't have ScrapBean?

Yes, from your computer. Save a copy as a zipped web page that opens in any browser, as a PDF with one sheet per page, or as a PNG picture of a single page. From the phone, a scrapbook goes to someone who has ScrapBean.

What are Highlights?

On the phone, scrapbooks you have not opened for a few days come back along the top of the library, like stories in a chat app. Tap one and its pages play by themselves, with the scrapbook's song if it has one. You can switch Highlights off in Settings.

Can I use my own fonts, stickers and backgrounds?

Yes. In Customize you can import fonts (TTF, OTF, WOFF), pictures and SVG drawings to use as stickers, a picture as a page background, and a song for the scrapbook. Bring one file, several, or a whole folder. Your fonts, pictures and backgrounds are then offered in all your scrapbooks.

Does ScrapBean use AI?

No. The layouts come from plain arithmetic and a roll of the dice. Nothing you paste is sent anywhere to be analysed.

Is ScrapBean open source?

No. The app is free to use, but its code is closed. You can extend it with plugins, which anyone can write.

Are plugins safe?

Each plugin runs shut in a box of its own, with no access to your files and no way to use the internet. It does nothing until you press one of its commands, and the first time it wants to read or change anything of yours, you are asked. What you allowed can be taken back at any time. No software can promise that nothing will ever go wrong, but a plugin is given very little to go wrong with.

Can I write my own plugin?

Yes. A plugin is a folder with two files: a short description and a JavaScript file. Five working examples come with ScrapBean, including one that converts pictures. There is no plugin shop yet; you add a plugin from a folder on your computer.
